# CREOVA Creative Direction

## Direction A — Cinematic Culture

Full-bleed documentary photography, dark burgundy overlays, minimal copy, and slow image reveals. Strongest for emotional impact and event storytelling; risk is reduced information density and over-reliance on large hero imagery.

## Direction B — Experimental Editorial

Asymmetric columns, typographic annotations, photo captions, index-like project lists, and bolder transitions between cream and burgundy. Strongest for distinctiveness; risk is usability loss if the editorial gestures become too abstract.

## Direction C — Living Culture (selected)

A hybrid of cinematic photography and disciplined editorial structure. People and work lead, while concise context, location, and purpose annotations establish trust. Cards are reserved for actual grouped interactions; other content uses image sequences, split layouts, and index rows.

### Why this direction

- It grows directly from CREOVA’s authentic image archive.
- It keeps the site warm, human, and culturally specific without imitating a fashion portfolio.
- It supports services, evidence, community, and founder narrative equally well.
- It accommodates English and French copy without fragile compositions.

## Visual principles

1. **People before polish** — show real expressions and exchanges; avoid anonymous decorative crops.
2. **Context is part of the image** — captions identify the program or setting where public evidence permits.
3. **One dominant gesture per section** — an image, statement, or action, not all three competing.
4. **Scale is earned** — only the homepage gets the largest title role; interior titles remain quieter.
5. **Gold signals meaning** — use it for focus, metadata, and active state, not ambient decoration.
6. **Motion follows attention** — reveal hierarchy and state; never animate merely to fill space.

## Founder imagery direction

- Use one environmental portrait as the About-page anchor, cropped 4:5 with generous context around the subject.
- Use a second candid working image in the homepage founder section; avoid repeating the About hero.
- Use a three-frame editorial sequence on About: portrait, working detail, community interaction.
- Apply consistent warm grading and a subtle burgundy shadow/overlay only where text must sit over a photo.
- Do not use online stock photography. Use only supplied imagery or existing approved repository assets.
- Alt text describes the visible activity and context, not personality or inferred emotion. Example: `Justin Mafie speaking with participants during a CREOVA community program.` Decorative crops use empty alt text.
